Scenes: rules for your audio devices

A scene is a name, a few triggers and two ordered lists of devices, one for input and one for output.

The headset case. You connect AirPods and macOS flips your input to their microphone. ToneCue puts the scene's input back. No script, no delay hack.

The topmost scene wins

Order your scenes. The first one with an active trigger decides the devices. A default scene covers everything else, so a plain "best connected device wins" list works from day one.

Five kinds of trigger

A device connects, a calendar meeting runs (optionally only with a meeting link, with a few minutes of lead), an app is open or in front, a microphone is in use, or the clock is in a time window on chosen weekdays.

Bluetooth guard

When macOS flips the input to a headset that just connected, ToneCue switches it back. It works per scene, and you can turn it off.

It says why

The popover names the active scene and gives the reason in one sentence. A history of recent switches has an Undo for each.

Your pick wins

Choose a device by hand and automation pauses until the scene changes or you press Resume. ToneCue switches at most once per second, so it never fights you mid-call.

Mute from anywhere

A global shortcut mutes the microphone and follows the input device. Links like tonecue://scene/Meeting work from Shortcuts and scripts. There is no link that unmutes, on purpose.

See what is on, and why

Click the ToneCue item next to the clock. The popover shows the active scene, the reason and the devices in use. One click switches a scene or a device.

Scenes

Meeting starts, the right mic stays on.

Editor

Best device first.

Each scene keeps two ordered lists. ToneCue uses the first device in each list that is connected, so a missing headset falls through to the next choice instead of to silence.

Questions

The ones that come up.

Does it fight me when I pick a device myself?
No. When you choose a device by hand, automation pauses until the scene changes or you press Resume. ToneCue's own switches are never mistaken for yours, and it switches at most once per second.
Which permissions does it ask for?
Switching devices needs none. Calendar access is requested only if you add a meeting trigger, and ToneCue reads event times and whether an event has a meeting link. The shortcut that opens the popover asks for one permission the first time you use it. The mute shortcut needs nothing extra.
What does it send over the network?
A license check and an update check. There is no account, no analytics and no tracking.
macOS has a sound menu. Why not Option-click?
Option-click switches once. It does not repeat when your headset reconnects, when a meeting starts or when you plug in the dock. ToneCue does that part for you and lists what it did.
There are free apps that rank devices. What is different?
A ranked list knows which device you own. A scene knows what you are doing: a calendar meeting, an open app, a microphone in use, a time window. If one fixed order works for you, a free list is enough. If the right answer changes with the meeting, the dock or the hour, scenes fit better.
What happens after the 14-day trial?
You need a license to keep using ToneCue. One license is 9.99 euros once, no subscription, and covers two of your Macs.
Is this the old AudioPilot?
Yes. ToneCue 2 continues AudioPilot 1.x under a new name and reads its settings. AudioPilot 1.0 to 1.3 were open source; ToneCue is proprietary.
Which Macs does it run on?
macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon and Intel. It is signed and notarized by Apple.
